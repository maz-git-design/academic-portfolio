I am a **Research Assistant at Newcastle University**, working in the field of **microelectronics and energy-efficient computing architectures**. My research focuses on the design and implementation of **in-memory computing architectures for Edge Artificial Intelligence (Edge AI)**, with particular emphasis on memory-centric hardware, low-power VLSI circuits, and on-chip learning.

I hold an **MSc in Microelectronics: Systems and Devices from Newcastle University**, completed as a **Chevening Scholar**, and an Engineering degree in **Electronics and Computer Science from the University of Kinshasa (UNIKIN)**, Democratic Republic of the Congo.

## Research Interests

My research interests lie at the intersection of **electronic device modeling, integrated circuit design, computer architecture, and artificial intelligence**, with an emphasis on developing energy-efficient hardware for intelligent systems.

My principal research areas include:

- **In-Memory Computing (IMC):** SRAM-based computing architectures, memory-centric processing, and on-chip learning.
- **AI Hardware Accelerators:** Energy-efficient architectures for Edge AI, TinyML, and logic-based machine learning.
- **VLSI and Integrated Circuit Design:** Digital and mixed-signal circuit design, physical implementation, and power-performance-area optimization.
- **Emerging Memory Technologies:** FeFET-based memory, gain-cell eDRAM, and alternative memory architectures for computing.
- **Computer Architecture and Embedded Systems:** FPGA-based prototyping, hardware/software co-design, and embedded computing platforms.

## Current Research

At Newcastle University, my research investigates **training-aware SRAM-based in-memory computing architectures for Tsetlin Machines**, a class of logic-based machine learning models.

My work explores the implementation of **Tsetlin Automata state updates directly within memory structures**, with the objective of reducing data movement and improving energy efficiency during on-chip learning.

This research involves transistor-level circuit design, memory architecture exploration, circuit simulation, and hardware validation, with a particular focus on the trade-offs between **energy consumption, latency, silicon area, and scalability**.

## Academic Background

Before joining Newcastle University, I worked as a **Junior Lecturer and Teaching Assistant at the Faculty of Engineering, University of Kinshasa**, where I contributed to teaching and laboratory activities in electrical engineering, signals and systems, and control systems.

My academic and professional experience also includes software development, systems engineering, and technology innovation, providing a multidisciplinary foundation for research at the intersection of **hardware, computing, and intelligent systems**.

## Research Vision

My long-term research objective is to contribute to the development of **energy-efficient, scalable, and adaptive computing architectures** capable of supporting artificial intelligence directly at the edge.

I am particularly interested in bridging the gap between **emerging memory devices, integrated circuit design, and machine learning algorithms**, from device-level modelling and circuit design to architecture development and hardware prototyping.
